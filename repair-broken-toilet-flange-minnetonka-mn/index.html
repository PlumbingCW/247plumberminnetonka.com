Broken Toilet Flange Repair: Minnetonka, MN Homeowner Guide

A broken toilet flange can often be repaired with a stainless repair ring or replaced with a new flange, once you know whether the break is in the ring or in the pipe below it.

By the Minnetonka 24/7 Plumbing team. Published 2026-10-22. Updated 2026-10-22.

Call (952) 866-9120

What the flange does and how you know it broke

The flange is the ring that bolts the toilet to the floor and connects its outlet to the drain. A wax ring seals the gap between the two. When the flange cracks, the closet bolts lose their grip, the toilet starts to rock, and the wax seal breaks. Water or sewer gas then escapes at the base.

Typical clues are a toilet that moves when you sit, a damp ring on the floor after flushing, a faint sewer smell, or a floor that feels soft near the bowl. The soft floor is the one to take seriously. Slow leaks rot subfloor, and no repair ring will help if the wood below will not hold screws.

Closet flange work follows the Minnesota Plumbing Code, Minnesota Rules chapter 4714, and a new fixture connection may need a permit, so check your city's standards.

Signs the floor under the toilet is already damaged

Check these before you spend money on a ring. They decide whether a flange repair can hold.

  • A screwdriver sinks into the wood beside the flange with little pressure.
  • The floor dips or springs when you stand next to the bowl.
  • Vinyl or tile near the base is stained, cupped or lifting.
  • The ceiling below, or a basement joist, shows a brown stain.
  • The toilet has leaked a little after every flush for months.
  • Mold smell at the base that returns after cleaning.

A careful DIY repair when the pipe below is sound

This applies only to a toilet on a first-floor or basement slab that is stable, not a bathroom you suspect has water damage.

  1. Shut off, drain and lift

    Close the supply valve, flush, and sponge out the tank and bowl. Disconnect the supply line and remove the nuts on the closet bolts. A toilet is heavy, so use two people and set it on a towel.

  2. Clean and inspect

    Scrape off old wax, stuff a rag in the pipe to block sewer gas, and check the ring for cracks, rust and soft wood. Probe nearby subfloor with a screwdriver. If it sinks in, stop.

  3. Install the repair ring

    A stainless ring screws into the subfloor and sits over the broken flange. Use stainless screws that reach solid wood. A properly fitted ring should sit flat and tight against the floor.

  4. Reset with a new wax ring

    Pull the rag, set new closet bolts, and lower the toilet straight down without twisting. Press down evenly, snug the nuts alternately, and add a bead of caulk if you like. Test with several flushes.

When to stop and call a plumber

If you find a cast iron pipe flaking apart, a drain lower than expected, or wood that crumbles, stop. These jobs mean cutting into the drain line or rebuilding the floor. Plumbing contractors in Minnesota communities over 5,000 must be licensed, and you can verify one at the DLI Check a License lookup.

A toilet that keeps leaking after a fresh wax ring may also have a drain problem. A slow drain or a gurgle after flushing points to a blockage downstream, and a toilet repair visit can sort out the fixture and the line in one trip.

Parts and tools worth having on hand

Buy a stainless repair ring or a spanner flange sized to your drain. Most homes use a four-inch pipe. You will also want new closet bolts, a wax ring with a plastic horn only if it suits your setup, stainless screws, a putty knife, and a cheap pry bar for stuck bolts. A wet vac helps with the last cup of water in the bowl.

Have an extender ring ready if the flange sits below the floor. It is a small plastic part that saves a second trip. Wear gloves. A toilet outlet is not a place for bare hands.

Why a flange fails on an older bathroom

Flanges crack from age and from a toilet that was never anchored well. Plastic flanges become brittle, brass bolts snap, and cast iron rusts at the ring. A bathroom retiled over the original floor is a special case, because the added tile can lift the floor above the flange so the seal never sits properly.

A wobble that is ignored becomes a leak, and a leak becomes a subfloor repair. Fixing the flange within a few weeks of the first rocking is much cheaper than fixing the damage that follows.

Testing the repair before you walk away

Flush five or six times and watch the base with a dry paper towel pressed around it. Check the ceiling or basement below for a drip. Rock the bowl with your hands. Any movement means the ring is not seated or the bolts are not tight, and the seal will fail again.

Come back the next morning and look again. A slow seep often shows up only after the wax settles. If the toilet still moves, or the smell returns, a plumber can check for a cracked pipe below the flange with a drain camera.

What changes the price of the repair

A flange fix costs more when the floor is damaged, when the drain pipe is cast iron that must be cut, when access is cramped, or when tile has to come up. A quick ring-and-wax repair is the small end. A rebuilt subfloor with a new closet flange and fresh finish is the large end.

If a bathroom is being redone, a plumbing installation visit can reset the flange at the right height before the new floor goes in, saving a second teardown later.

What Minnetonka homeowners should know about permits for toilet work

Rules differ across the lake. The City of Wayzata's page says it will not require permits, testing and inspection for plumbing fixture replacements, including toilets, only if the work does not alter the plumbing piping systems. Replacing the flange and drain pipe changes piping, so that exemption may not apply.

The City of Minnetonka plumbing permit application says work is not to start without a permit and lists no exemptions. So ask the City of Minnetonka building department before cutting into drain pipe. Homeowners doing their own work must own and occupy the home, which the form calls homestead. In Wayzata and Hopkins, contact the local office.

Source: City of Wayzata permit page and City of Minnetonka plumbing permit application.
