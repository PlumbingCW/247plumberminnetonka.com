Deep winter: the pipe in the cold wall

A Minnesota winter finds the weak spot in every house. It might be a supply line in an exterior wall, a hose bib left connected, or a pipe in an unheated garage. A frozen pipe shows up as a faucet that trickles or gives nothing while the others work. Open that tap, keep the heat on, and shut the main if you see a bulge or frost on the pipe.

The real damage often arrives at the thaw, when ice melts and a split pipe starts spraying. That is a burst pipe repair call, and the main shutoff is your best tool until the plumber arrives. Holiday cooking adds a second winter problem: grease packs kitchen lines, and a partly blocked drain is the first to fail when guests are over.

Plymouth's Building Inspection Division enforces the Minnesota State Building Code, and plumbing work is held to the standards in Minnesota Rules chapter 4714.

Spring thaw and the sump pit

Snowmelt and spring rain raise the groundwater around basements. A sump pit collects that water and a pump sends it outside. Pumps that sat idle all winter can stick, float switches can jam, and a discharge line can sit frozen or loose. Test yours: pour a bucket of water into the pit. The pump should start and empty it.

If it hums without moving water, or never starts, call for sump pump repair before the next heavy rain. Homes beside Medicine Lake, Parkers Lake or any wetland sit close to open water, so groundwater can rise faster there, and it is worth asking a plumber about pump capacity and a battery backup.

Summer storms and drains that cannot keep up

Hard summer rain can overload storm drains and push water into basement floor drains. Warm months also push tree roots toward the moisture in sewer lines, and a joint that was tight in winter can be packed with roots by August. When a floor drain backs up during a storm, stop using fixtures and call.

Drain cleaning with a machine cable handles most clogs. A hydro jetter scours grease and root hair off the pipe wall, which is the better fit when the line keeps slowing down. Drain cleaning and sump pump repair requests are welcome from 55446 and 55447, and from 55441 and 55442 as well.

Fall means boiler and hot water prep

Start your boiler in early fall, before the first cold night, not during it. Listen for banging, look for drips at the relief valve and check that radiators heat evenly. A call at midnight in the first hard freeze is when everyone else calls too. Boiler repair covers hot water heating systems.

A furnace is a different trade. We do not service forced-air furnaces, so call an HVAC company for those. Fall is also the time to disconnect garden hoses, drain outdoor spigots and test the sump pump again before the ground freezes.

Plymouth grew fast, and the pipes show it

Plymouth grew from 31,615 residents in 1980 to 81,026 in 2020, mostly before 2000. That puts a large share of homes at a generation old or more, so original water heaters, sump pumps and drain lines are reaching replacement age. In neighborhoods such as Mission Ponds, the Enclave development or the City Center district, ask when your home was built before deciding between repair and replacement.

Before we arrive at your Plymouth home

The right first step depends on the season, but these four apply year-round.

Find the main shutoff

In most Plymouth basements the main valve is on the wall where the water line enters. Learn its spot before winter, so you are not hunting by flashlight.

Kill power to wet areas

If the sump pit is overflowing or the basement is wet, turn off breakers for that area. Do not walk through standing water near outlets.

Clear the area

Move stored items off the basement floor and away from the drain or pit. Open the garage door or clear the walk if snow is deep.
