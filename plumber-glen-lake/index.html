Plumber in Glen Lake, Minnetonka, MN

Glen Lake is the neighborhood and shopping corridor around Excelsior Boulevard in Minnetonka, where single-family streets, townhomes, condos and apartments sit close together. This page explains what that mix means for plumbing calls and what to check before you phone.

What Glen Lake is, and what no source defines

The City of Minnetonka's 2014 Western Glen Lake Corridor guidelines describe Glen Lake as the neighborhood and commercial area around Excelsior Boulevard, where Eden Prairie Road and Williston Road meet it. The name comes from the lake just southeast of that intersection.

The city document gives no formal boundary, and this page does not invent one. Glen Lake Park sits at 5240 Glenview Drive, which is a handy landmark when you describe where you live.

Some of the history is still visible. A general store opened in 1907 at Excelsior Boulevard and Eden Prairie Road, and streetcar and freight tracks ran through Glen Lake from 1881 until service ended in 1932. For a plumber the point is simpler: older lots and newer buildings share the same blocks, so two nearby addresses can need very different questions.

Clogs, leaks and water heaters in a mixed neighborhood

This is general plumbing guidance and not a finding about any Glen Lake address. A slow drain that affects one fixture is usually a local clog. When several fixtures slow down together, the blockage is more likely in the main line, and drain cleaning with a cable or jetter is the usual first step.

If the same drain clogs again, a sewer camera inspection shows whether roots, a sag or a break is the cause. A tank water heater that leaks from the tank itself is a replacement job, while one that only runs out of hot water may need water heater repair first.

In a townhome or condo, a clog in a shared stack backs up into the lowest unit. Tell your association or manager before anyone opens a wall, because the pipe may not be yours.

What the city says about Glen Lake redevelopment

The city's page describes three phases. They are the north half of the Glenhaven shopping center at Woodhill Road and Tree Street, the south side of Excelsior Boulevard between the Xcel substation and Woodhill Road, and land on Stewart Lane along Glen Lake. It also lists new streetscaping and a renewed Glen Lake plaza.

Whether street or utility work is happening near your address is a question for Minnetonka Public Works at 952-988-8400. This page cannot tell you, and a drain or water problem that started during nearby construction deserves that call.

Source: City of Minnetonka Glen Lake redevelopment page.

Before you call about a Glen Lake plumbing problem

A few minutes of preparation shortens the call.

Find the shutoff

Locate the main water shutoff in the lower level or utility area. In a townhome or condo, ask which valve is yours.

Check responsibility

Owner, tenant or association member? Say which on the call, because it decides who can approve work.

Describe the symptom

Say which fixtures are affected, when it started, and whether water is still running.

Take photos

Take one wide shot and one close shot of the problem area before you call.
