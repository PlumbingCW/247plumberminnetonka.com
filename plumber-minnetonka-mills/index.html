Plumber in Minnetonka Mills, Minnetonka, MN

Minnetonka Mills takes its name from the mill built on Minnehaha Creek in the 1850s. Today it is a residential area with a historic house museum, three city parks and mostly midcentury homes. This page covers plumbing help for those streets.

Call (952) 866-9120

The mill that gave the area its name

The Minnetonka Historical Society says St. Anthony businessmen Simon Stevens and Calvin Tuttle decided in 1852 to build a sawmill at the rapids on Minnehaha Creek, and a dam was finished in early 1853. Thomas Perkins built a flour mill there in 1869, and it peaked around 1880 before it closed.

The Charles H. Burwell House at 13209 East McGinty Road dates from 1883, joined the National Register of Historic Places in 1974, and is run as a museum by the Minnetonka Historical Society.

None of that tells you about the pipes in a modern house. What it does show is that the creek is part of the area, so a wet basement here deserves a quick look at drainage before anyone assumes a pipe has failed.

Source: Minnetonka Historical Society and National Register of Historic Places.

Homes in the area

A Homes.com neighborhood profile describes Minnetonka Mills as mostly midcentury split-level and ranch homes, with a median build year of 1958 and typical lots of about half an acre. It lists ZIP 55305 and notes that some listings carry a Hopkins mailing address.

Treat those numbers as a rough description from a real estate site and not an inspection. The profile gives no boundaries, and neither does this page.

Homes with lower levels are where sump pumps, floor drains and water heaters usually live, so those are the most common things to ask about on a call. That is general guidance and not a claim about any one house.

When the water is coming from outside

A sump pump moves water that reaches the pit, but it cannot control how much arrives. Gutters, downspouts and yard slope decide that, and a plumber cannot regrade a yard. If the basement only floods in heavy rain, check those first and call if the pump itself struggles or stops.

What a first call covers

The call is short when you have these ready.

  1. Say where you are

    Give your street and a nearby landmark such as Minnetonka Mills Park or Big Willow Park.

  2. Describe what you see

    Say which fixtures are affected, when it started and whether water is still running.

  3. Name the shutoff

    If you know where the main shutoff is, say so. If water is spreading, close it first.

  4. Send a photo if asked

    A clear photo of the pump, heater label or wet area saves a second call.
