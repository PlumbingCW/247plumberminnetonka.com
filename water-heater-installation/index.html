Water Heater Installation in Minnetonka, MN

Water heater installation in Minnetonka covers setting a new unit where none stood before, or moving and upgrading an existing one, with gas, venting, water and permit work done to code. Call the 24/7 line to plan the job.

Projects that call for a planned install

Most water heater work starts with a failure. A planned installation starts with a decision, and these are the situations where it applies.

New construction

A new home needs the heater sited, vented, gas piped and inspected as part of the build. A City of Minnetonka Certificate of Occupancy will not be issued until Public Works confirms water meter installation, so the schedule matters.

Remodels and additions

Adding a bathroom or a kitchen can outgrow the old tank. We look at the extra demand and size the heater for it, rather than hoping the old one copes.

Relocating the heater

Moving a heater out of a finished space, or into a utility closet, changes piping, venting and drain paths. That is a design job before it is a plumbing job.

Upgrades and fuel changes

Switching from electric to gas, gas to heat pump, or tank to tankless means new connections and sometimes new circuits or gas capacity. See the tankless water heater page for that path.

Planned swaps before failure

Replacing an old tank on your schedule avoids the flooded floor. If the unit has already failed, emergency replacement is the faster route.

Gas piping and venting come first

On a gas unit, two decisions shape everything else: whether the gas line has capacity for the new appliance, and where the exhaust will go. An old half-inch line serving a furnace, a range and a heater can be short on supply, and we check that before ordering a unit.

Venting depends on the model. A standard atmospheric unit uses a draft hood and a metal flue to a chimney or vent. A high efficiency unit may vent with plastic pipe through a side wall, and it drains acidic condensate that needs a neutralizer or an approved drain path.

In Minnetonka, fuel gas piping needs a city license and state bond, and it uses a mechanical permit application, separate from the plumbing permit. We handle both as part of a gas heater job, and we leak test every connection before we leave.

Pressure, expansion and discharge piping

Heating water makes it expand. In an open system the extra volume flows back into the city main, but a check valve or pressure reducing valve on the incoming line closes that path. The result is pressure spikes, a weeping T&P valve and early wear on fixtures.

An expansion tank on the cold supply solves it by giving the water somewhere to go. We set its air charge to match your house pressure, which we read at a hose bib.

The T&P valve discharge pipe is the safety exit. It should be a full-size line pointed toward the floor or a safe drain, with no cap, no plug and no upward run. Minnesota Plumbing Code (Minnesota Rules chapter 4714) has a section on water heater requirements, and we install to it.

Unlicensed installs cause the worst failures

A bad water heater install can leak gas, back-draft exhaust into the house or burst a tank. Homeowners are sometimes offered a cheaper job from someone with a truck and no license, and the savings disappear the first time something goes wrong.

Check any plumber at the Minnesota DLI Check a License lookup (dli.mn.gov/license-and-registration-lookup). Minnesota requires a plumbing contractor license for plumbing work in communities over 5,000 residents, and the responsible individual must hold a master plumber license.

Permits and inspection in Minnetonka

Permit rules vary by town, so ask the building department for your address.

  • The City of Minnetonka plumbing permit application says work is not to start without a permit, and it lists no exemptions.
  • Fees are based on project valuation, which counts materials, equipment and labor. Residential permits start at $77 for valuations of $1 to $1,000.
  • Plan review, re-inspection and state surcharge fees may apply on top.
  • A homeowner who applies for their own permit must own and occupy the property.
  • For a home outside Minnetonka, such as one in Eden Prairie, ask that city. Eden Prairie lists water heaters among its permit items.

How a planned install goes

Planned jobs run better with a short walkthrough first.

  1. Walk the site

    We look at the space, current fuel, vent path and gas capacity, and agree on the unit.

  2. Permit and schedule

    We handle permit paperwork where we pull it, and book the install. Same-day appointments are available when the schedule allows.

  3. Install and connect

    Piping, venting, valves, the expansion tank and the discharge line go in together.

  4. Test and inspect

    We pressure test, check combustion and venting, and meet the inspector when one is required.
