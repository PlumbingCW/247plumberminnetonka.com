Basement Waterproofing in Minnetonka, MN

Basement waterproofing in Minnetonka is the set of fixes that keep groundwater and surface water out of a below-grade space. Call the 24/7 line to sort out whether your problem is a plumbing one.

Why Minnetonka basements take on water

Basement waterproofing means stopping groundwater and surface water from entering a below-grade space, or moving it out fast once it arrives. Much of Minnetonka's single-family housing was built between 1950 and 1970, and many of those homes have basements with original floors, cracks and older drain tile. So a home of that age is a good candidate for a closer look, not a reason to panic.

Minnehaha Creek runs through the city and the eastern tip of Lake Minnetonka lies within it. If your home sits near either, the soil around the footing may stay wetter, which makes a working sump pit and good drainage more important.

Liquid water or humidity: figure that out first

Basements feel damp for two different reasons, and the fixes do not overlap. Liquid water shows up as puddles, wet carpet edges or stains that darken after rain. Humidity shows up as condensation on cold pipes, a musty smell and white mineral deposits on the wall.

A simple test is to tape a square of plastic to a bare wall for a couple of days. Moisture under the plastic comes through the wall, while moisture on top is room air. Humidity is a dehumidifier and a ventilation job, not a waterproofing one.

The main waterproofing methods

Each method treats a different path for water to enter.

Interior drain tile

A channel is cut in the floor along the wall and filled with perforated pipe and stone. Water drains to a sump pit and out. It handles rising groundwater, not an exterior leak.

Sump pit and pump

The pit gives groundwater a place to go and the pump sends it outside. If the pump is the weak link, read about fixing a failed pump.

Exterior excavation

The soil is dug away, the wall sealed and drain tile replaced at the footing. It is the most invasive method and usually handled by a foundation or excavation contractor.

Crack sealing

Epoxy or polyurethane injection stops water through a single crack. It does not fix the pressure that forced the water there.

Window wells and gutters

A clogged well drain or a downspout dumping beside the foundation can be the whole problem. These are the cheapest fixes and the first to try.

Gutters, grading and the cheap fixes first

Before anyone digs, look outside. Downspouts should carry roof water several feet from the foundation, and the soil should slope away from the house. A clogged gutter that spills at the same corner every storm can mimic a foundation leak.

After a rain, walk the perimeter and watch where water pools. If the wet spot inside lines up with a downspout or a low spot, you may have found the source. Fix that first and see whether the basement stays dry through the next storm.

What a plumber does, and what a foundation contractor does

Plumbers handle the drainage side. That means sump pits and pumps, discharge lines, floor drains, drain tile tied to the sump, and finding out whether a sewer or supply leak is the real source. We use a drain camera and a pressure test to rule out a hidden leak or a backed-up line.

Foundation contractors handle the structure: wall cracks, exterior excavation, waterproof membranes and heavy sealing. We do not do structural foundation repair. If your problem is a bowing wall or an active crack, that work belongs with a foundation specialist, and we will tell you so.

Check for a plumbing leak before you waterproof

A wet basement is sometimes a plumbing problem. A leaking supply pipe, a failed water heater or a weeping sewer cleanout can look like groundwater. If the water appears when no rain has fallen, it may be a plumbing source.

A leak test can settle that. Watch the water meter with every fixture off, and if it moves, a pipe is leaking. Our leak detection page explains the tools we use to locate hidden pipe leaks.

What drives basement waterproofing cost

Waterproofing jobs vary widely. These are the cost drivers.

Length of drain tileCutting and rebuilding the floor along one wall costs less than the full perimeter.
Interior or exterior workDigging outside needs excavation, restoration and often a separate contractor.
Pump and pit sizeA larger pit, a battery backup or a second pump adds equipment and labor.
Finished spaceCarpet, drywall and built-ins have to be removed and replaced, which adds time.
PermitsAsk the City of Minnetonka building department whether the work needs a permit.

Standard homeowners policies often exclude groundwater seepage and sump failure unless a water backup rider was added. Read your policy and call your insurer, and do not expect waterproofing itself to be covered.
