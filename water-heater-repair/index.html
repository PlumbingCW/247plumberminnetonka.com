Emergency Water Heater Repair in Minnetonka, MN

Leaking water heater or gas smell: do this first

A water heater that is actively leaking or a unit with a gas smell is an emergency, and we take those calls around the clock. A tank can move a lot of water onto a basement floor in an hour, and a gas leak is a safety problem before it is a plumbing one.

If you smell gas, do not touch switches or the phone inside. Leave the house, then call the gas utility or 911 from outside. Call us after the gas company has cleared the home so we can look at the unit safely.

  1. Turn off the cold water supply valve on top of the heater. It is usually a lever or round handle on the pipe running into the tank.
  2. Switch the heater off. On an electric unit, flip its breaker. On a gas unit, turn the gas control dial to off or pilot, unless you smell gas, in which case you leave first.
  3. Move boxes, stored items and anything electrical away from the pooling water.
  4. If water is coming out of the discharge pipe beside the tank, leave the valve alone and call. Do not cap or plug that pipe.
  5. Take a photo of the data label and the leak area so the dispatcher can plan parts for a same-day visit.

Where the water is really coming from

A puddle near a water heater does not always mean the tank has failed. Water runs downhill and follows pipes, so the wet spot can be a foot from the true source. Condensation on a cold tank, a dripping supply line above, a bad shutoff valve or a drain valve that does not seat can all fake a tank leak.

There are three common sources. A fitting leak is a drip at a union, a nipple or a flexible connector, and it is usually a repair. A T&P valve discharge means the safety valve on the side or top of the tank is opening and sending water down the discharge pipe, which points to excess pressure or heat, or to a valve that has worn out.

A tank leak is water seeping from the steel shell itself, often from the bottom seam or around the element ports.

The tank leak is the one that ends the unit's life. The glass lining and the anode rod protect the steel, and once corrosion cuts through, no patch will hold. Tightening or sealing a rusted tank only delays a larger spill. If the shutoff is closed and water still appears under the heater a few minutes later, treat it as a tank failure and plan for replacement.

Finding the source takes ten minutes with a dry rag, a flashlight and a moisture meter. We wipe every joint, run the unit through a heat cycle, and watch which spot wets first. If the leak runs along a pipe or under a slab, leak detection with acoustic listening or a thermal imaging camera tells us where to open the wall.

Water heater requirements are covered in section 4714.0504 of the Minnesota Plumbing Code, Minnesota Rules chapter 4714, and pressure relief valves are covered in section 4714.0608, the standards licensed plumbers follow.

No hot water right now

Cold showers in a Minnesota winter are an urgent call, and we treat them that way. Same-day appointments are available when the schedule allows, and the line stays open overnight and on weekends. Before you call, check two quick things: the breaker or gas dial, and whether the tank is actually leaking.

A heater with no hot water and a wet floor needs the supply shut off first. A heater that is simply cold and dry usually has a failed heating part, a tripped limit switch or an ignition problem. Those are repairs we often finish in a single visit when the part is on the truck.

Gas, electric and hybrid units fail in different ways

The repair depends on how your heater makes heat, so the first thing we read is the data label.

Gas tank units

Faults center on the pilot assembly, thermocouple or flame sensor, the gas control valve and the vent. A weak or yellow flame, soot, or a backdrafting vent needs attention right away, because combustion gases are involved.

Electric tank units

Two heating elements and two thermostats do the work. A failed upper element leaves you with no hot water, while a failed lower element gives a short supply. We test each with a meter before replacing anything.

Hybrid (heat pump) units

These pair a compressor and fan on top with backup elements. Repairs include condensate drain clogs, air filter issues, fan faults and element failures. Some compressor and sealed-system work belongs to the manufacturer's service channel, and we say so when that applies.

Tankless units

Scale on the heat exchanger, a dirty inlet filter and error codes are the usual causes. See our tankless water heater page for flushing and sizing.

What a repair visit includes

A repair call is a diagnosis first and a parts job second. A technician will work through these steps in order.

  • Read the data label for model, age and fuel type, then check the venting and gas connection on gas units.
  • Test the thermostat, elements or ignition parts with a meter instead of swapping parts by guess.
  • Check the T&P valve, the discharge pipe and the water pressure at a nearby hose bib.
  • Look at the expansion tank, because a failed one pushes pressure up and keeps the T&P valve dripping.
  • Inspect the anode rod condition and sediment level when a flush or rod swap makes sense.
  • State the repair price before work starts, so you can decide on repair or replacement with the full picture.

When repair is not worth it

A tank that leaks from the shell is finished, whatever its age. A unit near the end of a typical tank lifespan, with a failing element and a rusted base, is a poor repair bet. Parts for a gas control valve on an old heater can approach the cost of a new tank, and the next failure is often close behind.

We tell you plainly which side of the line your heater is on. If replacement is right, the same visit can plan sizing, venting and the permit question. See water heater installation for what a planned install involves.

Licensed plumbers, and how to check any plumber

Water heater work touches gas, water and venting, so it belongs to licensed people. In Minnesota, plumbing work in communities over 5,000 residents requires a contractor license, and you can verify any plumber at the Minnesota DLI Check a License lookup. Minnetonka has about 54,000 residents, so that rule applies here.

Ask the City of Minnetonka building department whether your work needs a permit. Eden Prairie lists water heaters among its permit items, and Minnetonka's plumbing permit form states no exemptions, so assume a permit question is real.
