Urgent

Burst pipe, sewer backup or leaking water heater right now

Water on the floor does not wait for business hours, and neither does this line. Call the 24/7 line first, then use the steps below to limit the damage while a licensed plumber is lined up for your address.

The goal is simple: stop the water, protect the electrical side of the house, and keep people away from anything that came up through a floor drain.

  1. Shut off the main water valve for a burst pipe or a leaking supply line. It is usually on the basement wall where the water line enters the house, or in a utility closet in a townhome or condo.
  2. For a leaking water heater, close the cold water valve on top of the tank and switch the heater off at the breaker. For a gas unit, turn the gas control to pilot or off.
  3. Stay out of standing water if outlets, a panel or appliances are near it. Turn off power to that area from the breaker only if the panel is dry and you can reach it safely.
  4. For a sewer backup, stop running water. Do not flush, do not run the washer, and do not use the dishwasher until the line is cleared.
  5. Open a few faucets on the lowest floor after the main is off to drain the pipes and relieve pressure, and move rugs, boxes and electronics out of the wet zone.
  6. If you smell gas or hear a gas leak, leave the house and call 911 first. Call the plumbing line from outside.

The 24/7 emergency-first services we dispatch

These are the problems that make people call at night, on a holiday or in the middle of a cold snap. Each one has its own page with causes, method and cost factors.

Burst and frozen pipes

A split line can dump gallons a minute into a ceiling or crawl space, so a burst pipe repair starts with isolating the break and ends with a clean, permanent fix.

Clogged and backed-up drains

When a kitchen sink, tub or floor drain will not move, drain cleaning with a cable machine or a jetter reaches the blockage instead of pushing it deeper.

Water heaters that leak or go cold

A pooling tank or a heater with no hot water is a job for water heater repair, and a tank past saving moves on to replacement.

Main sewer line trouble

Gurgling toilets, slow drains on every floor and sewage at the lowest drain point usually point to the main line, where sewer line repair begins with a camera look.

Basement water and sump failures

A pit that overflows during a thaw or a heavy rain needs sump pump repair before the next cycle, not after the carpet is wet.

Hidden leaks

Water bills that climb, warm spots on a floor or a wet wall with no visible source call for leak detection using listening gear and thermal imaging.

Minnetonka housing and what it means for your plumbing

The City of Minnetonka's housing chapter counts about 23,825 homes. Roughly 56% are single-family detached houses, 29% are units in buildings of 20 or more apartments and condos, and 10% are townhomes. That mix changes who needs what: a house owner usually has a basement floor drain, a tank water heater and a sump pit to worry about, while a property manager cares about shared stacks and a building shutoff.

About 53.4% of Minnetonka homes were built before 1980, and much of the single-family stock dates from 1950 to 1970. Homes from that era often still have original drain lines and older supply piping, so a sewer camera inspection is a sensible first step before anyone quotes a repair on a recurring backup.

The city runs its own water system from 18 wells and published a service line inventory in 2025: no lead lines, and 3,907 lines of unknown material. If your address is one of those, a plumber who opens the line for a water service line repair can see the pipe material first hand. For questions about the inventory itself, ask the City of Minnetonka utilities staff.

Plumbing work in Minnesota is done to the Minnesota Plumbing Code, Minnesota Rules chapter 4714, and the state's licensing standards come from the Minnesota Department of Labor and Industry.

How a call to the 24/7 line works

The process is short on purpose, so you can get back to dealing with the water.

  1. Call the line

    A dispatcher answers at any hour, including nights, weekends and holidays. You do not need to know the plumbing term for what is happening.

  2. Describe the problem

    Say where the water is, what you already shut off and what type of building you are in. Photos are not needed, a clear description is.

  3. A licensed plumber is dispatched

    The dispatcher assigns a licensed plumber who can handle that kind of job and gives you the next step. Same-day appointments are offered when available for work that is not an emergency.

  4. Clear price before work starts

    The plumber looks at the problem, explains the options in plain words and states the price before any work begins. You decide whether to go ahead.

  5. Work and cleanup

    The plumber completes the repair, tests it and cleans the work area. Anything that needs a permit is handled with the City of Minnetonka building department.

How emergency plumbing is priced

No two emergencies cost the same, and any number quoted without seeing the problem is a guess. These are the factors that move the price.

What is actually brokenA failed supply hose is a small repair. A split copper line behind a finished wall or a collapsed sewer pipe is a larger one.
AccessOpen basement piping is quick to reach. Pipe inside a slab, a ceiling or a tight crawl space takes more time and sometimes drywall repair afterward.
Parts and materialsPipe, fittings, valves and fixtures vary by type and size, and a water heater swap depends on tank size and fuel.
Time and equipmentA drain camera, a hydro jetter or leak listening gear adds scope to a job. After-hours work is priced for the situation, and the plumber tells you before starting.
PermitsSome work needs a city permit. Ask the City of Minnetonka building department whether your project does.

Many home policies cover sudden burst-pipe water damage but not gradual leaks or routine maintenance. Read your policy and call your insurer, and keep photos of the damage.

Check any plumber's licence before you agree to work

Minnesota requires a plumbing contractor license for plumbing work in communities over 5,000 people, and the company's responsible individual must hold an active master plumber license. You can check any plumber or company yourself at the Minnesota Department of Labor and Industry's Check a License lookup.

Ask for the licensed company's name when the dispatcher calls back, search it on the DLI site and compare. It takes a minute and protects you whether you call this line or anyone else.
