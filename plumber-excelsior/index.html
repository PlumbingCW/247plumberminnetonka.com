Water Street and The Commons: old buildings, old lines

Downtown Excelsior packs shops, restaurants and apartments along Water Street, with The Commons and the Port of Excelsior at the lakefront. The Southwest Regional LRT Trail also runs through town. Many of these buildings predate modern plumbing, and the 1899 Excelsior Public School shows how old the town is. Assume original pipe until a plumber proves otherwise.

For a shop or an apartment above one, the useful prep is simple. Know where the shutoff is, who owns the building's drain and whether a floor drain exists. Dispatch will ask. What sits behind the plaster of a Victorian house

Victorian-era houses were plumbed, re-plumbed and added onto over more than a century. Behind a wall you may find galvanized steel supply lines clogged with rust, cast iron drains, and a patchwork of copper and plastic repairs. Rust-narrowed pipes cause low pressure, while worn joints cause slow seeps that rot framing before anyone sees a stain.

That is why leak detection leads the list here. An acoustic ground microphone and a thermal imaging camera locate a leak in a plaster wall within a small area, and a pressure test tells us whether the hot or cold side is losing. The alternative is cutting open original plaster to guess.

When leak detection points to a full repipe

One leak in an old line is rarely the only weak spot. If detection finds a second or third problem, or the camera and pressure test show rust-narrowed pipe throughout, repiping replaces the supply lines with new copper or PEX. Press fittings make joints without a torch, which matters in a wooden house with dry framing.

Repiping can happen in phases, floor by floor, with the water on for most of the day. The decision comes down to how many failures the tests find, how accessible the walls are and whether the house will be opened for other work anyway. If a pipe has already burst, a burst pipe repair visit comes first.

After the 2026 boil advisory: what to check in your plumbing

Excelsior had a boil water advisory in 2026 after a loss of pressure in the distribution system, and residents were told to flush their plumbing afterward. We do not diagnose health effects of water, so follow city and health guidance on that. These steps look at the pipes themselves.

  • Run each cold tap for a few minutes, then flush the hot side too, as the city advised.
  • Replace filter cartridges in refrigerators, pitchers and under-sink units that sat in the affected water.
  • Flush the ice maker and water dispenser lines before using them again.
  • Look for new drips at old connections, since a pressure drop and return can stress worn fittings.
  • Check the water heater base and relief valve for moisture a day later.
  • Report orange staining, because the city plant removes iron and manganese by gravity filtration and should not produce it.

Finishing a basement bath triggers that rule, so ask the city before you start.

Before we arrive at your Excelsior building

Old buildings reward a little advance work.

Find the main shutoff

In a Victorian house the valve may be in a stone-walled cellar or behind a panel. If it is stiff or corroded, do not force it. Tell dispatch.

Kill power to wet areas

If water is coming through a ceiling, switch off breakers for the rooms below and avoid light fixtures that hold water.

Clear the area

Move furniture and rugs off wet plaster or floors. Clear a path to the cellar and to any panel that hides plumbing.
